## Mathematical review and tool use

Mathbox's installed `proofread-math` skill and checklist guided final consistency
review. Two synthesis reviewers independently read all three new prompts. Their
corrections clarified replay versus first-publication accounting, lifecycle
timestamps, JSON-LD parity, optional-method assumptions, strict runtime authority,
late adjudication and scope-proportionate repository reading.

The prompt now distinguishes public-exposure events E(D) from distinct qualified,
authorized canonical first publications P(D). Reactivations are outside P(D).
Replay/backlog can enter P(D), but not its fresh-discovery subset. Primary cohorts
are exclusive; secondary provenance is retained. This is an analytical reporting
clarification, not a database migration or retroactive rewrite of accepted data.

For the uncertainty example, a local calculation of the exact one-sided
zero-event binomial bound `1 - alpha^(1/n)` with `alpha=0.05`, `n=50` gave
**0.05815508**. Under independent representative Bernoulli sampling, zero errors
in 50 observations therefore does not demonstrate a 1% ceiling. The corresponding
zero-error sample sizes for upper bounds no greater than 1% and 0.5% are 299 and
598. These illustrative calculations do not amend accepted sample requirements.
Method context: [NIST proportion intervals](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm).

Little's law uses compatible long-run averages; a coefficient-of-variation cutoff
is a local heuristic, not a universal theorem assumption. The prompt separates
that identity from observed service capacity and finite-window latency.
Reference: [MIT queueing lecture](https://web.mit.edu/1.041/www/lectures/L8-queuing-models-2026sp.pdf).
