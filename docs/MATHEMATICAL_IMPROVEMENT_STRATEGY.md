## How a mathematical challenge is resolved

Every challenge must carry a problem statement, baseline, units, estimand,
assumptions, hard constraints, data/lineage, candidate methods, simplest baseline,
falsifiable hypothesis, cost budget, experiment, success/failure criteria,
uncertainty, implementation mapping, deployment/observation evidence and rollback.

Use the least complex method that meets the measured requirement. A proven
deterministic rule can resolve a challenge when an advanced optimizer adds no
material benefit. An algebraically correct equation is not implementation;
passing fixtures are not representative production acceptance.

The plan separates investigation outcome from operational resolution:

- A hypothesis may be supported or rejected with evidence.
- A method may be retained, replaced or rejected.
- A challenge is operationally resolved only when its underlying requirement
  passes acceptance through a validated solution or an accepted simpler baseline.
- An evidence gap, resource constraint, source-permission block or unmet product
  target remains visible with an owner, next action and trigger. It is not a
  solved challenge. A scoped external limitation may be accepted through the
  existing governance process, but must remain distinct from resolution.

Program completion requires a defensible disposition for all thirteen challenges,
cross-control integration checks, current evidence and explicit unresolved
limitations. Never compress this into a misleading “13/13 solved” when some are
deferred or constrained.

## Measurement, objectives and safeguards

Keep safety, source authority, leases, opt-outs, canonical identity, data rights
and accepted quality/resource bounds as feasibility gates. Within the feasible
set, follow the accepted objective ordering and compare Pareto tradeoffs before
introducing scalar weights. Define units and normalization for every cost/benefit.

Track stock, canonical first publication, fresh first publication, replay,
reactivation and backlog separately. Use complete Manila days for the daily
floor and compatible time boundaries for rates. Current source state must not
rewrite original publication evidence; publish versioned corrections when later
adjudication changes a result.

Do not confuse `P(not PH | published)` with conventional false-positive rate
`P(predicted eligible | actually ineligible)`. Name the estimand and use the
matching independent sample and denominator. An empty or inadequate sample is
UNKNOWN/INSUFFICIENT, not a passed ceiling.

The accepted project constraints govern production. Attachment values such as
decay 0.95–0.99, a 3-day half-life, 0.88 merge threshold, PI gains, 360–400 inventory,
or CUSUM 4–5 remain hypotheses until calibrated and accepted for the affected use.
Trigger.dev examples do not authorize reviving the historical runtime.

## After the challenges are resolved

Transition from building controls to operating and renewing them:

1. Re-measure fresh supply, quality, marginal source value, queue residence,
   cost, dependency risk and recovery performance on their approved schedules.
2. Detect model/policy/data drift; reopen only the affected challenge with fresh
   evidence and an accountable next step.
3. Exercise failure recovery, source replacement, independent adjudication,
   restore and fresh-maintainer replay. Preserve a minimal deterministic mode.
4. Evaluate the 100/day floor against the full accepted observation window. If
   unmet, select the next evidenced feasible supply constraint; report a measured
   market/resource limit when that is the result. Mathematical program completion
   is distinct from the supply target and from autonomous cutover acceptance.
5. Remove experiments or complexity that did not earn their operational cost.
   Preserve negative results so later maintainers do not repeat failed approaches.
